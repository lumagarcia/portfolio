Featured Project

Can machine learning predict employee climate before the next survey?

Predicting year-over-year changes in climate and engagement survey favorability, and uncovering which dimensions act as organisational levers.

Data3 annual survey cycles · 2023–2025
Scale22,293 valid responses · ~9,000 employees
Scope35 items · 9 dimensions
MethodsRegression, Ridge, Random Forest, time-series validation
StackPython · Pandas · Scikit-Learn · Matplotlib
Executive summary · 30-second read

I don't just build models. I tell the business whether to trust them, and what to do next.

The challenge

Companies run annual climate surveys and react to the results. Can HR instead anticipate where employee sentiment will move, and know where to invest to lift the whole climate?

What I found

With three survey cycles, a simple "nothing changes" baseline beat Random Forest, Ridge and Linear Regression, so I said so instead of forcing a model. The nine dimensions, however, are strongly interdependent (mean R² 0.70), and three of them lift the rest.

What I recommended

Invest in autonomy and process efficiency, act directly on pay and well-being, and hold off on predictive AI until there is a longer data history.

What this shows about how I work
  • Rigour Strict time-based validation with no data leakage, benchmarked against a naive baseline.
  • Honesty A negative result reported clearly, which protects the business from a costly wrong bet.
  • Business sense Statistical findings turned into three concrete HR actions.
  • Scale 22,293 survey responses, 35 items, 9 dimensions, from raw data to recommendation.

Large, consistent samples each year (roughly 7,300 to 7,600 respondents out of ~9,000 employees) make the cross-sectional analysis robust.

The limitation is the time dimension: three cycles give only two year-over-year transitions, which is why the temporal models struggled.

How I approached it

1Standardise35 comparable items across 3 cycles
2Define targetFavorability = share of scores 4–5; Δ in p.p.
3ModelCross-sectional and temporal paths
4ValidateStrict time split, no look-ahead leakage
5RecommendTranslate results into HR actions
Methodology details (for the technically curious)
Path A · Cross-sectional
  • Each of the 9 dimensions modelled as the dependent variable against the other 8 in the same cycle.
  • 70% training / 30% test split.
  • Multiple Linear Regression vs. Random Forest, compared via R² and RMSE, plus feature importance.
Path B · Longitudinal
  • Train on Δ 2023→2024, test on Δ 2024→2025.
  • Algorithms: Naive, OLS, Ridge, Random Forest.
  • GridSearchCV with TimeSeriesSplit; evaluated by MAE, RMSE, R² and Pearson correlation against the naive baseline (Δ = 0).
Dataset

Anonymised secondary data from a Climate & Engagement Survey at a Brazilian technology company with roughly 9,000 employees. Five-point Likert scale, consolidated into nine dimensions:

Culture of IntegrityOrganisational AgilityResponsible LeadershipHealthy EnvironmentEngagementOrganised ProcessesFair RewardsAttractive CareersThriving Individuals

What the business should do

01

Invest systemically

Concentrate on autonomy, ownership and process efficiency (Thriving Individuals, Organised Processes). Gains spill over into leadership, engagement and agility.

02

Act directly on pay & well-being

Perceptions of compensation, benefits and work-life balance won't improve as a side effect of other initiatives. They need specific interventions.

03

Govern people data wisely

Avoid adopting predictive AI without a long history of structured data. For short- and medium-term decisions, historical stability is the safest baseline.

Proposed next steps

Collect additional survey cycles to give the models more transitions, and test whether team- or business-unit-level data improves forecasts beyond the stability baseline.
